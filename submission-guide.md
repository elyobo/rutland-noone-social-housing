## Understanding Clause 52.20

Before preparing your submission, it's helpful to understand the planning pathway this proposal uses and how it differs from standard planning processes.

### What is Clause 52.20?

[Clause 52.20](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/52.20) is a planning provision titled "Government Funded Housing Development" that creates a streamlined approval pathway for social housing projects wholly or partly funded by the Victorian or Commonwealth Government and delivered by the Chief Executive Officer of Homes Victoria.

### Who decides?

The Victorian Minister for Planning makes the decision, not the local council (City of Yarra). There is no right of third-party appeal to VCAT. The Minister's decision is final.

### What does it override?

[Clause 52.20](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/52.20) overrides most planning controls that would normally apply, including:

- Zone height limits (in this case, the 9m mandatory maximum in [General Residential Zone Schedule 2](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/32.08-s2))
- Zone garden area requirements (35% minimum)
- Standard residential development codes (ResCode [Clause 54](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/54), [55](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/55)) and Better Apartments Design Standards ([Clause 58](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/58))

The clause does not override certain overlays (Environmental Audit, City Link Project, Melbourne Airport Environs, etc.), but none of these apply to this site.

### What standards apply instead?

The clause substitutes its own 42 discretionary development standards covering street setbacks, permeability, car parking, overshadowing, overlooking, noise, wind impacts (for 5+ storey buildings), communal open space, accessibility, and many others.

**Critically, there is no building height standard in the 42 requirements.** Building height is entirely at the Minister's discretion under the integrated decision-making framework ([Clause 71.02-3](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/71.02-3)).

### The height question explained

The site is zoned [General Residential Zone Schedule 2](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/32.08-s2) (GRZ2). GRZ Schedule 2, Section 4.0 states:

> "A building used as a dwelling, small second dwelling or residential building must not exceed the height of 9 metres."

This is a mandatory limit (not a guideline), with four narrow exemptions (extending existing building, matching adjacent buildings, rebuilding damaged building, building under pre-existing permit). None of these exemptions apply to this development.

The schedule also includes a decision guideline (Section 6.0) asking whether opportunities exist to avoid buildings being "visually obtrusive" through alternative building designs, "particularly for developments with overall building heights in excess of 9 metres." This presupposes that buildings exceeding 9m require specific design consideration to avoid visual obtrusiveness.

**How Clause 52.20 overrides the height limit:**

[Clause 52.20-1](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/52.20-1) states "The provisions of this clause prevail over any inconsistent provision" and [Clause 52.20-2](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/52.20-2) provides a blanket exemption from zone controls. This combination legally sets aside the 9m GRZ2 height limit. However, [Clause 52.20](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/52.20) contains no replacement height standard. The 42 development standards cover setbacks, overshadowing, overlooking, permeability, parking, noise, wind, and many others, but not maximum building height.

This means:
1. The zone height limit is set aside by the exemption ([52.20-2](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/52.20-2))
2. No replacement height limit is imposed by Clause 52.20
3. The proponent must address the zone height in their report ([52.20-5](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/52.20-5))
4. The Minister decides whether the proposed height is acceptable under integrated decision-making ([Clause 71.02-3](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/71.02-3))
5. There is no VCAT review

The height of a Clause 52.20 project is entirely a matter for the Minister's discretion, with no hard cap.

**The proposed building:**

The proposed building is 8 storeys and 26.5m (28.65m with rooftop plant), which is 2.94 times the 9m zone limit. The properties directly across Rutland Street are zoned [NRZ1](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/32.09-s1) (Neighbourhood Residential Zone Schedule 1), the most restrictive residential zone, and are in a Heritage Overlay with contributory grading. The zone transition from 26.5m GRZ2 to single storey heritage NRZ1 across one residential street is extreme.

### What must the applicant address?

While the zone controls don't legally bind, [Clause 52.20-5](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/52.20-5) requires the applicant to submit a report addressing "the purposes, objectives, decision guidelines or statements of significance or risk of any zone, overlay, or other provision that would apply to the use or development were it not for the exemptions."

This means the applicant must explain how the proposal responds to zone purposes, the Municipal Planning Strategy, heritage policies, local building heights policies, and neighbourhood character expectations. The Minister then decides whether to approve using integrated decision-making ([Clause 71.02-3](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/71.02-3)) that balances competing objectives: social housing delivery against neighbourhood character, heritage, and built form policies.

### The "equal or better" test

Victorian Government guidance states the development standards "should be met but can be varied at the discretion of the Minister for Planning provided that an equal or better alternative overall design outcome is achieved." This test applies to each departure from the standards. Note that this is government policy guidance, not a statutory requirement in Clause 52.20 itself.

## Key Principles

### Focus on the planning policy framework, not just opposition to height

The strongest arguments demonstrate conflict with specific planning scheme provisions:

- **MPS mid-rise direction** ([Clause 02.03-4](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/02.03-4)) — directs mid-rise to activity centres, employment areas, major regeneration areas, and boulevards, not residential neighbourhoods
- **Local building heights policy** ([Clause 15.01-2L](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/15.01-2L)) — reinforces MPS direction with an enumerated list of appropriate mid-rise locations
- **Heritage interface context** ([Clause 15.01-1L](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/15.01-1L)) — requires street wall heights "no higher than adjoining contributory heritage buildings"
- **Incremental change area strategies** ([Clause 16.01-1L](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/16.01-1L)) — requires development to respect "fine-grain subdivision pattern, neighbourhood or streetscape character and identified heritage significance"
- **Boulevard transition policy** ([Clause 15.01-1L](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/15.01-1L)) — requires development to transition down in scale from boulevards into residential hinterlands

These are provisions the proponent is required to address under [Clause 52.20-5](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/52.20-5).

**The boulevard transition issue is particularly significant.** The site sits at the boulevard transition zone, fronting a service road separated from Alexandra Parade by infrastructure barriers and substantial grade separation. The boulevard transition policy explicitly requires development to transition down in scale from boulevards into residential hinterlands. The proposal intensifies at the interface (8 storeys) then drops abruptly to single-storey heritage houses one street into the neighbourhood.

### Acknowledge the social housing need

Submissions that support redevelopment in principle but seek modifications to scale are more persuasive than blanket opposition. A submission that says "we support 86-105 dwellings at 6 storeys" is harder to dismiss than one that says "the building is too big."

The housing need is genuine. The choice isn't between 114 dwellings and zero — it's between different scales of redevelopment that deliver substantial new social housing while better respecting planning policy.

### Request specific modifications, not just rejection

The Minister can approve with conditions. Requesting specific modifications gives the Minister concrete options for achieving better planning outcomes while still delivering substantial new social housing.

**Five suggested modifications in two tiers** (detailed in `proposal-analysis.md`):

**Tier 1 (achievable without building shift):**
1. **Reduce height to 6-7 storeys** — reduces zone height override from 2.9× to 2.2-2.6×, improves heritage interface, still delivers 86-100 dwellings (2.9-3.3× multiplier vs existing 30)
2. **Relocate vehicle access to Noone Street** — achieves [Clause 52.20-7.18](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/52.20-7.18) compliance (crossover location maximising pedestrian safety), eliminates vehicle/pedestrian conflicts at underpass exit directly opposite current proposed crossover, removes 105 daily vehicle movements from heritage-facing Rutland Street. Can be achieved using existing 9.555m western gap.
3. **Increase Rutland setback to 4.0m minimum** — eliminates current 1.45m shortfall, achieves standard compliance

**Tier 2 (subject to AS5100 risk assessment confirming feasibility):**
4. **Westward building adjustment** — enables generous Rutland setback (6-12m), optimal vehicle circulation, enhanced landscaping, and potential yield recovery to 95-108 dwellings through wider floor plate. Building well-positioned (lot boundary 11.92m from stabling siding, 1.92m past the 20m threshold but 8.08m from the 10m collision zone). Requires AS5100 risk assessment before construction.
5. **Enhanced landscaping with canopy trees** — uses generous setback space (6-12m) from westward adjustment for substantial deep soil planting and urban greening

These modifications work together in tiers: Tier 1 delivers 86-100 dwellings (2.9-3.3× multiplier) with planning compliance improvements; Tier 2 delivers 95-108 dwellings (3.2-3.6× multiplier) with generous setbacks and enhanced landscaping. The Minister can approve with a condition requiring an AS5100 assessment before construction, with the building shifting west if the assessment confirms feasibility.

### Identify the errors of fact

The Planning Report contains **six factual errors**. Four systematically understate the degree to which the proposal departs from standards on matters central to the proposal (height, setbacks). Two additional errors (vehicle access road naming and landscaping table row citation) demonstrate a broader pattern of carelessness and consistently citing less stringent provisions:

**Error 1: Building height — 17.57m claimed, actual 26.5m**

The report states (p. 41) "The maximum building height of 17.57m exceeds this preferred maximum height" in the section addressing GRZ2 height compliance. The actual maximum building height is **26.5 metres** (28.65m with rooftop facilities). The 17.57m figure doesn't match any building element and understates the building height by nearly 9 metres.

**Error 2: GRZ2 height limit — claimed 11m default, actual 9m mandatory**

This error appears twice in the Planning Report, creating an internal contradiction. Page 28 quotes the 9-metre limit correctly then introduces the 11-metre fallback as if it applies. Page 41 flatly states "No maximum building height is specified in Schedule 2 of the General Residential Zone and therefore this proposal is subject to a discretionary maximum height of 11m."

Both are incorrect. [GRZ Schedule 2](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/32.08-s2), Section 4.0 states: "A building used as a dwelling, small second dwelling or residential building must not exceed the height of **9 metres**." The 11-metre fallback from [Clause 32.08-11](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/32.08-11) only applies where a schedule does NOT specify a height limit. GRZ2 does specify 9 metres, so the fallback does not apply. The actual zone height control is 9 metres mandatory, not 11 metres discretionary. This makes the exceedance 2.9x instead of 2.4x.

**Error 3: Visual obtrusiveness decision guideline not addressed**

GRZ2 Schedule 2 decision guidelines (Section 6.0) explicitly ask: "Whether opportunities exist to avoid a building being visually obtrusive through the use of alternative building designs, particularly for developments with overall building heights in excess of 9 metres."

At 26.5m (2.9 times the 9m threshold), the proposal is well beyond what the guideline contemplates. The guideline specifically asks about "alternative building designs" — a 6-7 storey building is precisely such an alternative.

Under [Clause 52.20-5](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/52.20-5), the proponent must address the zone's decision guidelines. The Planning Report doesn't address this guideline — the most directly relevant GRZ2 guideline for an 8-storey proposal.

**Error 4: Rutland Street setback — claimed 3.45m at ground floor, actual 2.55m**

The report claims a Rutland Street setback of 3.45m "at ground floor and Level 1" (pp. 16 and 48). The architectural drawings show the 3.45m figure is correct at Level 1, but at ground floor the dimension is **2,550mm (2.55m)** from the lot boundary to the building face (ground floor plan TP2100, Design Package p. 17).

[Clause 52.20-6.2](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/52.20-6.2) requires "walls of buildings" to meet the setback standard, without distinction by level. The actual setback is 2.55m against a required 4.0m, a variation of **1.45m (36% below standard)**, not the 0.55m (14% shortfall) the report's figures imply.

**Error 5: Vehicle access road — claimed "Alexandra Parade", actual Alexandra Parade East**

The report states (p. 7) "vehicle access from Alexandra Parade." This is incorrect. Vehicle access is from **Alexandra Parade East**, a separate 10-metre-wide service road with no direct connection to Alexandra Parade at this location. The two roads are physically separated by pedestrian/cycling paths, mature trees, sound barriers, and vertical grade separation. Saying "vehicle access from Alexandra Parade" implies direct arterial road access, a significant transport advantage. Access is actually from a service road, with vehicles required to travel via local residential streets (Noone Street/Rutland Street) to reach the site.

**Error 6: Landscaping table row — cited "1501-2500 square metres", actual "2501 square metres or more"**

The Planning Report (p. 67) cites the "1501-2500 square metres" row of Table 5 for landscaping requirements when the site is 3,247 m². The correct row is "2501 square metres or more", which requires 15% deep soil rather than 10%. The development appears to comply with the correct (more stringent) standard, so this error does not affect the landscaping outcome. It does, however, add to the pattern of errors that consistently cite less stringent provisions than those that actually apply.

**Inadequate compliance assessments:**

Beyond the factual errors above, the Planning Report fails to adequately assess certain compliance requirements:

**Vehicle crossover location ([Clause 52.20-7.18](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/52.20-7.18)):** The standard requires "The location of crossovers should maximise pedestrian safety." The Planning Report's assessment (p. 78) addresses only pedestrian access to the building, not whether the crossover location on Alexandra Parade East satisfies the pedestrian safety standard. The applicant's own Design Report Part 1 (p. 14) maps an existing pedestrian/cyclist underpass with its exit directly opposite the proposed crossover. Neither the Traffic Engineering Assessment nor the Planning Report acknowledges or assesses this interface. The crossover location has not been assessed against the explicit pedestrian safety requirement. See detailed framing in "Key Compliance Issues to Raise" section.

**False compliance claims:**

The Planning Report also contains compliance claims that are contradicted by the applicant's own design documents:

**Car parking security ([Clause 52.20-6.6](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/52.20-6.6)):** The report claims (p. 51) car parking "Be secure" by stating "All access to the car park is via a secure entry point at the south from Alexandra Parade East." However, the architectural drawings show the car park is an open under-croft at ground level:
- South elevation (Design Package p. 30): Annotated "OPEN TO CARPARK"
- West elevation (Design Package p. 31): Multiple "OPEN TO CARPARK" annotations along railway corridor
- Design Report Part 2 (p. 43): Explicitly calls it "open air car park"
- Landscape Design Report (p. 30): Labels it "Covered Carpark" while reserving "secure" for bicycle storage

An open under-croft accessible from a railway corridor and freeway service road, with no physical access control beyond landscaping (explicitly described as avoiding "harsh boundaries"), does not meet the standard meaning of "secure" for residential car parking. This claim demonstrates the Planning Report asserts compliance with standards the design plainly does not meet, raising questions about the reliability of compliance claims across all 42 development standards.

**Missing supporting documents:**

The Planning Report (p. 27) references wind advice from Windtech Consultants but the wind assessment report itself is not among the submitted documents. For an 8-storey building in a low-rise residential area, ground-level wind effects at the Rutland Street and Noone Street interfaces are a legitimate amenity concern. The absence of this report prevents independent verification of the claim that wind impacts are acceptable.

**Why this matters:**

Submissions that identify factual errors, inadequate assessments, and false compliance claims in the applicant's materials carry weight. The factual errors aren't matters of interpretation, they're errors of fact that should be corrected regardless of the merits of the proposal. The inadequate assessments (like the crossover location) and false claims (like car parking security) demonstrate that the [Clause 52.20-5](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/52.20-5) obligation to adequately address all relevant provisions has not been discharged. This is a matter of procedural integrity, not just substantive planning merit.

### Address the "equal or better" test

Victorian Government guidance states [Clause 52.20](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/52.20) standards "should be met but can be varied at the discretion of the Minister for Planning provided that an equal or better alternative overall design outcome is achieved."

The 8-storey proposal cannot deliver an "equal or better" outcome compared to GRZ2 requirements:

- **Height**: The zone mandates 9 metres. The proposal is 26.5 metres (2.9× the limit). No design measure (materials, setbacks, articulation) changes the fundamental height relationship with the 9-metre zone or the single-storey heritage houses opposite.

- **Heritage interface**: GRZ2 anticipates 3-storey development at most. The heritage policy requires street wall heights "no higher than adjoining heritage buildings" (approximately 6 metres). An 8-storey building creates a 4.4:1 height ratio across the street that no podium or tiered setback strategy can reconcile with these standards.

- **Height override at zone interface**: The planning scheme contemplates GRZ2 adjacent to NRZ1, but with GRZ2 at its 9m limit. A 26.5m building (2.9× the zone limit) facing single-storey heritage NRZ1 houses is unprecedented in the Clause 52.20 register.

The additional 28 dwellings from the top two floors (bringing total from 86 to 114) should be weighed against these departures. A 6-storey building at the same footprint could deliver 86–94 dwellings, or with westward expansion 95–105 dwellings, while materially improving planning policy alignment.

### The Minister's decision isn't reviewable at VCAT

There's no third-party review right. The submission is the only opportunity to put arguments on the record.

### Vehicle Crossover Location and Pedestrian Safety ([Clause 52.20-7.18](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/52.20-7.18))

The proposed vehicle crossover on Alexandra Parade East is located directly opposite the exit of an existing public pedestrian and cyclist underpass that passes beneath the railway line. This underpass is identified as active pedestrian and cyclist infrastructure on the applicant's own Green Travel Infrastructure map (Design Report Part 1, p. 14). Alexandra Parade East has been identified by the City of Yarra as part of a safe alternate cycling route in the Clifton Hill area.

Neither the Traffic Engineering Assessment (Traffix Group, January 2026) nor the Planning Report (Tract, January 2026) acknowledges or assesses the interaction between the proposed vehicle crossover and this underpass exit. The Traffic Engineering Assessment contains no mention of the underpass in its 25 pages, despite including detailed sections on cycling infrastructure (p. 10) and walking infrastructure (p. 11). The Planning Report's [Clause 52.20-7.18](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/52.20-7.18) compliance statement (p. 78) addresses only pedestrian access to the building from Rutland and Noone Streets. It does not address the impact of the crossover location on existing public pedestrian and cyclist movements.

[Clause 52.20-7.18](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/52.20-7.18) of the Yarra Planning Scheme (p. 1051) requires that "The location of crossovers should maximise pedestrian safety and the provision of on-street visitor car parking and on-street bicycle lanes."

Locating a vehicle crossover generating approximately 105 daily movements (Traffic Engineering Assessment p. 18: 35 spaces with standard residential trip generation rates) directly opposite a known pedestrian and cyclist arrival point, without any assessment of the resulting conflict, does not satisfy this standard. The applicant mapped the underpass themselves, then failed to assess whether the crossover location complies with the explicit pedestrian safety requirement.

**Personal observations:** [Insert your own observations about the underpass: gradient, visibility conditions, typical usage patterns, cyclist speeds on exit, specific safety concerns based on local knowledge. For example: "As a regular user of this underpass, I observe that cyclists exit at speed due to the downhill gradient, with limited visibility of turning traffic. The underpass is well-used during peak commute times..." etc.]

**Proposed solution:** Relocating the vehicle crossover to Noone Street would eliminate this conflict entirely. The development's northern podium has a 9.555m gap from the western boundary that can accommodate vehicle access without any building shift. This relocation would:

- Satisfy [Clause 52.20-7.18](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/52.20-7.18) by removing the crossover from the pedestrian/cyclist conflict point
- Improve pedestrian and cyclist safety at the underpass exit
- Remove all 105 daily development-generated vehicle movements from Rutland Street (the heritage-facing frontage)
- Simplify vehicle access by avoiding the circuitous three-turn path through residential streets currently required for Alexandra Parade East access
- Require no third-party approvals (uses only land within the site and existing public road)

At minimum, the Minister should require a supplementary traffic engineering assessment addressing the crossover/underpass interface before determining whether the proposed crossover location satisfies [Clause 52.20-7.18](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/52.20-7.18). The current assessment is incomplete.

## Appendix B: Glossary

**Change areas** - Yarra Planning Scheme classification: High change, Moderate change, Incremental change, Minimal change. This site is incremental change (not high/moderate where apartments are policy-supported).

**HCTZ** - Housing Choice and Transport Zone. New residential zone ([Clause 32.10](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/32.10)) introduced by Amendment VC257, applied to residential land within activity centre catchments. Allows 3-4 storeys standard, 6 storeys on large sites (1,000+ sqm, 20m+ frontage) in inner catchments. Not yet applied within the City of Yarra.

**Clause 52.20** - "Government Funded Housing Development" provision creating streamlined approval pathway for government-funded social housing, overriding most zone controls including height limits.

**Clause 71.02-3** - Integrated decision-making framework requiring the Minister to balance competing policy objectives in favour of net community benefit.

**GRZ2** - General Residential Zone Schedule 2. Applies to this site. Mandatory 9m height limit.

**GRZ3** - General Residential Zone Schedule 3 in Yarra ("Office of Housing Sites over 2500sqm & Selected Main Road Sites"). Wellington Street (closest precedent) is in GRZ3, distinguishing it from this GRZ2 site.

**NRZ1** - Neighbourhood Residential Zone Schedule 1. Most restrictive residential zone. Applies to heritage properties across Rutland Street.

## Submission Logistics

**Consultation period:** Check the Homes Victoria website or notice boards for the exact dates (typically 14 days under [Clause 52.20-4](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/52.20-4))

**How to submit:** Contact details should be provided in the consultation notice. Typically submissions go to the Minister for Planning via Homes Victoria or the Department of Transport and Planning.

**What happens next:** The Minister considers submissions but isn't bound by them. The consultation process under [Clause 52.20-4](https://planning-schemes.app.planning.vic.gov.au/Yarra/ordinance/52.20-4) is more limited than standard notice and review processes under Part 4 of the Planning and Environment Act, and the Minister has no statutory obligation to demonstrate how submissions were considered in the decision. However, submissions form part of the public record and may influence the Minister's assessment under the integrated decision-making framework. The Minister can approve, approve with conditions, or refuse the application. The decision isn't reviewable at VCAT.
