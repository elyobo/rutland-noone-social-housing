# Submission: Noone & Rutland Street Social Housing Development

**Re:** Clause 52.20 application for 11, 21 & 23 Rutland Street and 77 Noone Street, Clifton Hill

## Position

As a YIMBY Melbourne member, I'm in favour of the proposed redevelopment at this site, with some caveats as described below.

The existing 1950s building was in poor condition, unpleasant for both residents and other local residents. I'm glad to see more housing, especially social housing, and more residents coming to Clifton Hill. We have a housing crisis and a particular shortage of social housing that this project will help to address. Clifton Hill is a very low density suburb and some intensification is welcome as well. The location is well suited to such intensification, with good public transport and active transport connectivity. Generally the design and landscaping are good and are appropriate for the neighbourhood context.

**I request the Minister approve this project at 6 storeys with the modifications detailed below.**

A 6 storey building could still more than triple the housing on site while achieving materially better planning policy alignment than the proposed 8 storey building.

## Requested Modifications

I request the Minister approve this development with the following modifications:

### 1. Reduce height to 6 storeys

A 6 storey building matches the two tallest buildings in Clifton Hill (210 Alexandra Parade East and 122 Roseneath Street) that the applicant's own Planning Report cited as evidence of neighbourhood character. At 6 storeys, the height override ratio (2.07x) matches the only other GRZ2 precedent on the [Clause 52.20 government funded housing development list](https://www.planning.vic.gov.au/planning-approvals/Government-funded-housing-development) (High Street, Mount Waverley). The proposed 8 storeys would be the largest GRZ height override on record (2.94x, 19.5% higher than the next closest at 2.46x).

**Yield impact:** 6 storeys still roughly triples the housing on site compared to the existing 30 units, with westward adjustment potentially increasing yield through a wider floor plate.

### 2. Relocate vehicle access from Alexandra Parade East to Noone Street

The current design locates the vehicle crossover on Alexandra Parade East directly opposite a pedestrian and cycling underpass exit. The applicant's documents show this infrastructure on their site context maps (Traffic Engineering Assessment, pp. 7, 17; Design Report Part 1, p. 14) but neither the Traffic Engineering Assessment nor the Planning Report assessed the crossover/underpass interaction. This fails to satisfy Clause 52.20-7.18, which requires crossover locations to "maximise pedestrian safety."

Noone Street access would eliminate this safety conflict, remove all development-generated traffic from Rutland Street and Alexandra Parade East, and provide simpler vehicle circulation.

### 3. Achieve minimum 4.0m Rutland Street setback at all levels

Clause 52.20-6.2 requires a 4.0m front street setback. The ground floor plan shows the main building wall at 3.445m from the Rutland Street boundary, with an uncovered raised platform projecting to 2.555m. The Planning Report claims 3.45m as the "minimum setback" (pp. 16, 49).

The actual minimum is either 2.555m or 3.445m depending on whether the ground-level platform counts toward the setback. Either way, there is a shortfall against the 4.0m standard: 0.555m (14%) or 1.445m (36%). For an 8 storey building opposite single storey houses, any setback shortfall is significant.

### 4. Require assessment of rail proximity requirements before decision

The Design Report avoided assessing structural requirements for building adjacent to rail, constraining the building position based on uncertain AS5100 applicability. Multi-storey residential development immediately adjacent to rail is both feasible and has been approved within the City of Yarra (The Hub, 8 Garfield Street, Richmond: 9 storeys, 170 apartments, 2015, built immediately adjacent to rail embankment, though in MUZ zone in a denser development context), and the existing multi-storey 1950s buildings on site operated within the claimed constraint zone for ~70 years.

The applicant should have assessed structural requirements during design development to determine whether westward positioning is viable.

Shifting the building 2-9m west would enable:
- Generous Rutland Street setback (4.5-12m depending on shift magnitude), removing the need for the requested variation from the 4.0m standard
- Enhanced landscaping and deep soil planting
- Improved streetscape transition
- Potential to offset height reduction yield loss through a wider floor plate

The Minister should require an assessment of structural requirements for building adjacent to rail before making a decision to determine whether westward positioning is structurally and economically viable. If viable, the building should shift west to improve compliance, setbacks and landscaping, with a wider floor plate potentially offsetting some yield loss from height reduction. If prohibitive, the modifications 1-3 alone still deliver improved outcomes.

## Key Planning Concerns with 8 Storey Proposal

### 1. Unprecedented height override

The proposed 26.5m building is 2.94 times the zoned GRZ2 9-metre mandatory height limit for the site, 19.5% higher than the next closest Clause 52.20 GRZ precedent. The two closest precedents (Elizabeth Street, North Richmond at 2.46x and Wellington Street, Collingwood at 2.4x) are both in GRZ Schedule 3 specifically titled "Office of Housing Sites over 2500sqm & Selected Main Road Sites." Only 4 of 16 verified 5+ storey projects are in GRZ zones, so placing a large building here at all is unusual - the rest are in zones that anticipate higher-density development. It's worth pointing out that Wellington Street, the most comparable development, is situated next to much larger housing blocks and has substantially more open space available.

At 6 storeys (2.07x override), this development would match High Street, Mount Waverley, the only other GRZ2 project on the [Clause 52.20 government funded housing development list](https://www.planning.vic.gov.au/planning-approvals/Government-funded-housing-development), and match the tallest existing buildings in Clifton Hill that the applicant cited as local context. Matching rather than exceeding the established maximum by two storeys in a low rise neighbourhood better aligns with the "incremental change" area classification (Yarra Planning Scheme clause 16.01-1L).

### 2. Planning policy directs mid-rise elsewhere

The Municipal Planning Strategy (Clause 02.03-4) directs mid-rise buildings to activity centres, employment areas, major regeneration areas, and along boulevards. The Building Design policy (Clause 15.01-2L) requires mid-rise to "ensure the height of new buildings respond to the building height requirements set out in the planning scheme" This site:

- Is not in an activity centre, employment area, or major regeneration area
- Addresses Rutland Street (residential frontage) as its primary interface, not Alexandra Parade East (it is not on Aledandra Parade, the nearest boulevard, at all)
- Is in an "incremental change area" (not "high or moderate change" where apartment development is policy-supported)
- Exceeds the zone height requirement by 2.94x

### 3. Scale transition and setback

An 8 storey building (26.5m) opposite single storey Victorian terraces across Rutland Street creates a 4.4:1 height ratio at 15-20m distance. The ground floor setback falls short of the 4.0m standard, with measurements of either 2.555m or 3.445m depending on how a ground-floor element is characterised. Either represents a significant shortfall (14-36%) for an 8 storey building opposite single storey houses.

The applicant's own documents characterise a 2-4 storey to single storey transition as "dramatic" (Design Report Part 1 p. 17; Planning Report p. 15). The proposed transition is more than double that height differential.

A 6 storey building with 4.0m+ setback would provide a 3.3:1 height ratio with standard compliance, still large, but a more appropriate scale transition in line with the largest buildings in the area.

### 4. Planning Report contains six factual errors

Four errors systematically understate planning departures:

1. **Building height:** States 17.57m (Planning Report, p. 41), actual 26.5m (architectural drawings TP3000 & TP3001, Planning Report p. 16)  understated by 9m
2. **Zone height limit:** Claims no schedule height specified or cites 11m fallback (Planning Report, 41), actual GRZ2 mandatory limit is 9m
3. **Decision guideline:** Fails to address GRZ2 Section 6.0 decision guideline on visual obtrusiveness for buildings exceeding 9m
4. **Rutland Street setback:** Claims minimum setback of 3.45m (Planning report, p. 16, p. 49), but a ground-floor element projects to 2.555m from the boundary

Two additional errors demonstrate carelessness: vehicle access road misidentified as "Alexandra Parade" (Planning Report p. 7, actual: Alexandra Parade East), and wrong landscaping table row cited (Planning Report p. 67 cites "1501-2500 square metres" row for 3,247 sqm site, actual: "2501 square metres or more").

Under Clause 52.20-5, the applicant must adequately address zone purposes, decision guidelines, and all relevant provisions. When the report misstates the building height by 9m, incorrectly claims no schedule height limit exists, omits the visual obtrusiveness guideline, and overstates the minimum setback, the Clause 52.20-5 obligation has not been adequately discharged.

### 5. Crossover/underpass safety conflict not assessed

The applicant's documents show a pedestrian and cycling underpass on Alexandra Parade East with its exit directly opposite the proposed vehicle crossover (Traffic Engineering Assessment, pp. 7, 17; Design Report Part 1, p. 14). Neither the Traffic Engineering Assessment nor the Planning Report mentions or assesses this interaction. The Planning Report's Clause 52.20-7.18 compliance statement (p. 78) addresses only pedestrian access to the building, not the impact of the crossover location on existing public pedestrian and cyclist movements. Additionally, the Traffic Engineering Assessment is missing key data, with appendices B, C, and D being empty.

Clause 52.20-7.18 requires crossover locations to "maximise pedestrian safety." Locating a crossover generating approximately 105 daily movements (Traffic Engineering Assessment, p. 24) directly opposite a pedestrian/cyclist arrival point, without any assessment of the conflict, fails this standard.

### 6. Missing wind assessment

The Planning Report claims compliance with Clause 52.20-7.21 (wind impacts for 5+ storey buildings) based on Windtech Consultants advice (pp. 7 & 27, Appendix A p. 80), but the wind assessment report was not provided during community design consultation and is not among submitted documents. For an 8 storey building in a low-rise residential area, ground-level wind effects are legitimate amenity concerns. The absence of this report makes compliance unverifiable and prevented informed community submissions on wind impacts.

## Integrated Decision-Making (Clause 71.02-3)

The Minister must balance competing objectives under the integrated decision-making framework. The critical question is proportionality: does the marginal dwelling gain from the last two storeys (Levels 7-8) justify:

- The largest GRZ height override on the [Clause 52.20 government funded housing development list](https://www.planning.vic.gov.au/planning-approvals/Government-funded-housing-development)
- Conflict with multiple planning policies (incremental change classification, MPS mid-rise direction, scale transition)
- A 4.4:1 height ratio to single storey houses across one residential street
- Exceeding the tallest existing buildings in the neighbourhood

The marginal dwellings are important, but Homes Victoria delivers social housing across Victoria, including many projects within or near zone height limits. The question is not whether these dwellings should be built, but whether this specific site's planning context justifies the policy cost of building them here at 8 storeys rather than 6.

At 6 storeys, this development still roughly triples the existing housing stock while achieving better planning policy alignment.

---

## Conclusion

I request that the Minister require an assessment of structural requirements for building adjacent to rail before deciding, and approve this development at 6 storeys with vehicle access from Noone Street and minimum 4.0m Rutland Street setback. If the assessment confirms westward building adjustment is viable, this should be incorporated to improve setbacks, landscaping, and yield.

This delivers substantial social housing, maintains the reduced parking provision, and achieves substantially better planning policy alignment than the proposed 8 storey building along with improved outcomes for current and future residents in the proposed development and the surrounding neighbourhood.
