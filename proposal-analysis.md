# Noone & Rutland Street Proposal Analysis

## Introduction

The redevelopment of this site is overdue. The aging 1950s walk-up units are in poor condition, with negative impacts on residents and neighbours. With Australia's housing crisis and massive social housing waiting lists it makes sense to intensify at this location, with a large lot permitting a substantial build.

More housing and more residents in Clifton Hill are positive outcomes. The site's proximity to quality public transport and active transport infrastructure makes it a suitable location for moderate residential intensification. A substantial increase from 30 dwellings to roughly 90 (at 6 storeys without westward adjustment) or over 95 (with westward adjustment) would roughly triple or more than triple the housing on site while matching the scale of the tallest existing buildings in Clifton Hill.

The building design is considered. The material choices respond to the neighbourhood character and look good. The landscape strategy, including the retention of mature trees and the use of hardy, drought-tolerant native species, is sound. The all-electric, 4-star Green Star approach is appropriate.

The reduced parking provision is appropriate for this transit-rich, inner suburban location and should be maintained or even reduced further if necessary to facilitate further improvements as discussed below.

### Executive Summary

**Six key concerns:**

1. **Unprecedented height override.** The proposed 26.5m building is 2.94 times the 9m GRZ2 mandatory zone limit (GRZ Schedule 2), 19.5% higher than the next closest Clause 52.20 GRZ precedent. Only 4 of 16 verified projects of 5+ storeys are in GRZ zones; the rest are in zones that anticipate higher-density development.

2. **Planning policy directs mid-rise elsewhere.** The Municipal Planning Strategy (Clause 02.03-4, Built environment and heritage) directs mid-rise development to activity centres, employment areas, major regeneration areas, and boulevards. The Building Design policy (Clause 15.01-2L, Building design) requires mid-rise to respond to zone height requirements. The site is classified "incremental change" (Clause 16.01-1L, Location of residential development) where housing growth must be "commensurate with the purpose of the zone", not "high or moderate change" where apartment development is policy-supported.

3. **Scale transition and setback.** An 8 storey building would sit 15-20m from single storey Victorian terraces across Rutland Street (4.4:1 height ratio), with substandard setbacks. The applicant's own documents characterise a 2-4 storey to single storey transition as "dramatic" (Design Report Part 1, p. 17; Planning Report, p. 15); the proposed transition is more than double that height differential.

4. **Planning Report errors.** Six factual errors: building height stated as 17.57m (actual 26.5m, Planning Report p. 41; architectural drawings TP3000 & TP3001), incorrect zone height claim (stated as 11m or unspecified, actual 9m mandatory limit), omitted GRZ2 Section 6.0 decision guideline on visual obtrusiveness for buildings exceeding 9m, incorrect setback measurement (stated as 3.45m minimum, actual 2.555m or 3.445m depending on ground-floor element interpretation, ground floor plan TP2100), vehicle access road misidentified (stated as "Alexandra Parade", actual Alexandra Parade East, Planning Report p. 7), and wrong landscaping table row cited (Planning Report p. 67). The first four understate planning departures; the last two demonstrate carelessness.

5. **Crossover/underpass safety conflict not assessed.** The applicant's documents map a pedestrian and cycling underpass exit on Alexandra Parade East directly opposite the proposed vehicle crossover (Traffic Engineering Assessment, pp. 7, 17; Design Report Part 1, p. 14), but neither the Traffic Engineering Assessment nor Planning Report assesses this interaction. Clause 52.20-7.18 (Access) requires crossover locations to "maximise pedestrian safety."

6. **Missing wind assessment.** Clause 52.20-7.21 (Wind impacts) requires buildings of 5+ storeys to not cause unsafe wind conditions. The Planning Report claims compliance based on Windtech Consultants advice (pp. 7, 27, Appendix A p. 80), but the assessment report was not provided during the community consultation period and is not among the submitted documents, making compliance unverifiable and preventing informed community submissions on wind impacts.

**Preferred modifications:**

A 6 storey building with vehicle access from Noone Street and westward building adjustment would roughly triple or more than triple the existing 30 units (depending on the extent of westward adjustment) while delivering substantial improvements: falls within GRZ precedent range, matches the tallest existing buildings in Clifton Hill, eliminates underpass/crossover conflicts, improves Rutland Street interface with generous setback, and enables enhanced landscaping. The westward adjustment likely requires AS5100 risk assessment to confirm structural feasibility.

Six storeys without westward adjustment (86-94 dwellings) would also substantially improve the proposal, delivering a 2.9-3.1x multiplier over existing units.

Five modifications are proposed in two tiers (detailed in Part 3): Tier 1 (achievable without building shift) includes height reduction to 6-7 storeys, Noone Street vehicle access, and 4.0m minimum Rutland setback. Tier 2 (subject to AS5100 assessment before decision) adds westward building adjustment for optimal setbacks and enhanced landscaping.

The Minister can require AS5100 assessment before making a decision, with the outcome informing whether to approve with or without westward adjustment. If assessment identifies prohibitive costs, Tier 1 alone delivers defensible outcomes (roughly tripling existing housing). If assessment confirms feasibility, Tier 2 enables more than tripling existing housing with improved setbacks and landscaping.

### Finding 1: Unprecedented Height Override

**The zone height limit:**

The site is zoned General Residential Zone Schedule 2 (GRZ2). GRZ Schedule 2 states:

> "A building used as a dwelling, small second dwelling or residential building must not exceed the height of **9 metres**."

This is a mandatory limit, not a guideline. The schedule also includes a decision guideline asking whether buildings over 9 metres are "visually obtrusive". This guideline requires design consideration to avoid visual obtrusiveness for any development exceeding the mandatory height limit.

**The proposed height:**

The proposed building is **8 storeys and 26.5 metres** (28.65m with rooftop plant). This is **2.94 times the 9-metre zone limit**.

**What Clause 52.20 does:**

Clause 52.20 legally sets aside this zone height limit through its blanket exemption provision. But the clause doesn't replace it with any alternative height standard, there's no maximum height in the 42 development standards. Height is entirely at the Minister's discretion under the integrated decision-making framework (Clause 71.02-3, Integrated decision making).

**Precedent analysis:**

The [Big Housing Build Register (Clause 52.20 government funded housing development list)](https://www.planning.vic.gov.au/planning-approvals/government-funded-housing-development) lists 59 approved projects using Clause 52.20 as of early 2026 (register covers July 2021 to present). Most are small-scale (1-2 storeys in regional/outer suburban areas). Sixteen verified projects are 5 storeys or taller (excluding two discontinued or ambiguous projects). Of these 16, only 4 (25%) are in General Residential Zones (GRZ). The remaining 12 (75%) are in zones that explicitly anticipate higher-density development: Residential Growth Zones, Mixed Use Zones, Activity Centre Zones, and Commercial Zones.

Every project of 9 storeys or taller is in a non-residential zone:

- Holland Court, Flemington: 12 storeys (Mixed Use Zone)
- Egan Street, Carnegie: 10 storeys (Commercial 1 Zone)
- Essex Street, Prahran: 9 storeys (Activity Centre Zone)

**Methodological note:** Override multipliers below are calculated using current zoning and current height limits (as at February 2026). Zones and height limits may have changed since project approvals. These figures represent the scale of current non-compliance with current rules, not necessarily the override magnitude at the time of approval. Despite this limitation, the comparison provides a consistent basis for assessing relative scale across all precedent projects.

The tallest project in any residential zone is [Wellington Street, Collingwood](https://www.urban.com.au/news/collingwood-and-south-yarra-new-sites-in-victorian-government-s-5-3-billion-social-housing-project): 8 storeys, 26.4m in General Residential Zone Schedule 3, a Yarra schedule specifically titled "Office of Housing Sites over 2500sqm & Selected Main Road Sites." GRZ3 schedule specifies no height, so the 11-metre default applies (higher than GRZ2's 9m schedule limit), making the override 2.4 times the limit. The Wellington Street buildings are also located next to much taller developments and have substantially more open space available.

**Other confirmed residential zone height overrides:**

- [Bills Street, Hawthorn](https://www.boroondara.vic.gov.au/services/planning-and-building/planning/planning-scheme-and-amendments/victorian-government-planning-initiatives/public-housing-redevelopment-bills-street-hawthorn): Building G (tallest), 7 storeys, 22.5m in GRZ4 (2.05x the 11m default limit, measured from highest natural ground level to roof level per north elevation)
- [High Street, Mount Waverley](https://www.monash.vic.gov.au/Planning-Development/Proposed-Developments/Major-Developments/Proposed-development-plans-at-554-558-High-Street-Road-Mount-Waverley): 6 storeys, 18.6m in GRZ2 (2.07x the 9m schedule limit, measured from highest natural ground level to building high point per east elevation)

**This proposal would be the largest override:**

At 2.94 times the GRZ2 9-metre schedule limit, the Clifton Hill proposal would be the largest GRZ height override on the register, 42% higher than the next closest (Elizabeth Street, North Richmond at 2.46x). The two closest precedents, Elizabeth Street (2.46x) and Wellington Street (Collingwood, 2.4x), are both in GRZ3, a Yarra schedule specifically titled "Office of Housing Sites over 2500sqm & Selected Main Road Sites." High Street (Mount Waverley, 2.07x) is the only other GRZ2 project on the register.

Clifton Hill is also the only proposal that exceeds a schedule-specified mandatory height limit (9m in GRZ2). The other precedents exceed default limits (Elizabeth Street (North Richmond), Wellington Street (Collingwood), and Bills Street (Hawthorn) all exceed 11m defaults where their schedules specify no height).

At 8 storeys this would be the largest GRZ height override on record (2.94x). At 7 storeys (22.5m, 2.50x) it would still exceed all GRZ precedents. At 6 storeys (18.6m, 2.07x) it would exactly match High Street, Mount Waverley, the only other GRZ2 project on the register, and match the tallest existing buildings in Clifton Hill that the applicant cited as local context. Matching rather than exceeding the established maximum by two storeys in a low rise neighbourhood better aligns with the "incremental change" area classification (Clause 16.01-1L, Location of residential development).

### Finding 2: Planning Policy Directs Mid-Rise Elsewhere

**Municipal Planning Strategy (Clause 02.03-4, Built environment and heritage):**

The Yarra Planning Scheme is explicit about where mid-rise buildings should be located. Clause 02.03-4 (Built environment and heritage) states:

> "Reinforce Yarra's low-rise neighbourhoods by directing mid-rise buildings to appropriate locations, within major and neighbourhood activity centres, employment areas (as identified in clause 02.01), major regeneration areas (as shown on the Strategic Framework Plan in clause 02.04), and along boulevards (Hoddle St, Alexandra Parade, Victoria Parade and the south end of Queens Parade)."

The MPS also requires: "Ensure mid-rise buildings are in accordance with any building height requirements set out in the relevant zone or overlay."

**Building Design policy (Clause 15.01-2L, Building design):**

The local Building Design policy (Clause 15.01-2L, Building design) reinforces this:

> "Ensure that development reflects the predominant low-rise character of the area, except in the areas below where mid-rise should be directed" [then lists the same locations].

Where mid-rise development is appropriate, the policy requires it to:

> "Provide a transitional scale to the buildings in adjoining low-rise neighbourhoods to protect amenity and discourage visual bulk."

**The site context:**

The Noone & Rutland Street site:

- Is not in an activity centre, employment area, or major regeneration area
- Addresses Rutland Street (residential frontage) as its primary interface, not Alexandra Parade East (it is not on Alexandra Parade, the nearest boulevard, at all)
- Is in an "incremental change area" (not "high or moderate change" where apartment development is policy-supported)
- Exceeds the zone height requirement by 2.94x

**Strategic Housing Framework (Clause 16.01-1L, Location of residential development):**

The Planning Report (p. 36) states: "The Site is located within an 'Incremental change area'." The Strategic Housing Framework Plan (Clause 16.01-1L, Location of residential development) classifies land into different "change areas" based on anticipated growth:

- **High change areas**: Major activity centres, major transport nodes
- **Moderate change areas**: Neighbourhood activity centres, select main road sites
- **Incremental change areas**: Areas where some growth is appropriate but must respect existing character
- **Minimal change areas**: Areas where the existing low-rise character should be preserved

The development block is incremental change, while the surrounding residential areas (east of Rutland Street through to Hoddle Street) are minimal change.

For incremental change areas, Clause 16.01-1L (Location of residential development) requires development that:

> "Respects the fine-grain subdivision pattern, neighbourhood or streetscape character and identified heritage significance."

And:

> "Ensure housing growth in minimal change areas and incremental change areas outside activity centres are commensurate with the purpose of the zone."

The zone purpose (Clause 32.08, General Residential Zone) is "to encourage development that is responsive to the neighbourhood character of the area." The zone's mandatory height limit is 9 metres.

**Where apartment buildings are supported:**

Clause 16.01-1L-01 (Housing diversity) supports:

> "well designed apartment development in high and moderate change areas."

Not incremental. Not minimal.

**Assessment:**

An 8 storey building dropping abruptly to single storey houses one street into the neighbourhood provides limited transitional scale. The housing diversity policy supporting apartments applies to high and moderate change areas but this site is neither. An 8 storey, 26.5-metre building is not "commensurate with the purpose of the zone" in an incremental change area.

Design response measures (the choice of materials, the use of stepped building forms, the architectural expression) are ways of managing the impact of a chosen height. They don't explain why 8 storeys is appropriate for a location where planning policy explicitly directs mid-rise development elsewhere.

A 6 storey building would better align with the incremental change classification and locational policy framework while still delivering substantial housing (95-105 dwellings with westward adjustment, 86-94 without).

**Emerging policy context:**

Victoria's Housing Statement (The Decade Ahead 2024-2034) sets a target of 44,000 additional dwellings for Yarra by 2051. While this is a relevant strategic document the Minister will weigh in favour of housing delivery, the Statement itself acknowledges "heritage and character considerations will remain important" and directs increased density to activity centres and public housing renewal sites. A 6 storey alternative would still make a substantial contribution to these targets.

The Victorian Government's Train and Tram Zone Activity Centres Program has designated the City of Yarra as a "city-wide" activity centre to be addressed through a separate planning process. This does not mean the entire Yarra LGA has been reclassified as an activity centre under the current planning scheme. No planning scheme amendment has been gazetted. The Yarra Planning Scheme's activity centre hierarchy, change area classifications, and mid-rise location policies remain as adopted under Amendment C269yara (December 2023). The "city-wide" designation describes the geographic scope of a future planning process, not a current zone or overlay change.

The first areas being progressed within Yarra under this program are the existing activity centres in Fitzroy and Collingwood through Amendment C271yara. The program's standard height framework for catchment areas contemplates 3-4 storeys, rising to 6 storeys on large sites (1,000+ sqm with 20m+ frontage) in inner catchments. The program explicitly preserves existing heritage overlays. If anything, this reinforces the case for 6 storeys over 8: the government's own framework for residential catchment areas tops out at 6 storeys on large sites.

Clause 52.20-5 (Requirements for plans and documents) requires the applicant to address "zone purposes" in the design review report, indicating zone purposes remain relevant policy context even when controls don't bind. The integrated decision-making framework (Clause 71.02-3, Integrated decision making) requires the Minister to balance the zone purpose against social housing objectives.

### Finding 3: Scale Transition and Setback Analysis

**The proposed interface:**

The proposal places:

- An 8 storey building (26.5 metres to roof, 28.65m with plant, per architectural drawings TP3000 & TP3001)
- With a 4 storey street wall on Rutland Street (the Noone Street podium)
- Directly opposite single storey (approximately 6-metre) houses
- At a distance of approximately 15-20 metres across the residential street
- With a ground floor street setback that is either 2.555m (to uncovered raised platform) or 3.445m (to main wall) depending on interpretation, against a 4.0m standard. The architect labels the ground-floor element "BALCONY 002" though it is at ground level and uncovered. Whether this counts toward the setback is ambiguous, but either measurement falls short of the 4.0m standard.

The substandard setback brings the building mass closer to the Rutland Street streetscape than the development standards require (by 0.555m to 1.445m, depending on interpretation).

**The applicant's response:**

The Planning Report (pp. 40-41) addresses the neighbourhood context through design narrative: "tactile, fine-grained materials," "the rhythm of traditional terrace homes," articulated facades, and a material palette referencing Clifton Hill's industrial character.

**Assessment:**

The architectural design by Fieldwork demonstrates competent quality. The podium-and-tower form with articulated facades, the material palette (brick and metal screens), ground-floor activation along Rutland and Noone Streets, and the Level 4 communal garden represent considered responses to the site context. The 4-star Green Star Buildings rating target and all-electric design show commitment to environmental performance. The building's architectural treatment is appropriate for contemporary medium-density housing.

However, design quality does not resolve the fundamental scale relationship. These are design response measures that manage visual impact, but they don't address whether an 8 storey building with substandard setbacks provides an appropriate scale transition to single storey houses 15-20 metres away.

The Municipal Planning Strategy and local building design policy (Clause 15.01-2L, Building design) require mid-rise development to "provide a transitional scale to the buildings in adjoining low-rise neighbourhoods to protect amenity and discourage visual bulk." A 26.5-metre building opposite 6-metre houses across one residential street is a 4.4:1 height ratio with no intermediate stepping. Even with the podium setbacks, the entire building remains visible in the sight line of residents across Rutland Street.

[Victorian planning guidelines](https://www.urban-design-guidelines.planning.vic.gov.au/guidelines/buildings) support podium-and-tower forms in activity centres to manage bulk and create pedestrian scale. However, these design measures reduce but don't eliminate the fundamental scale mismatch. A 6-7 storey building with consistent setbacks would provide a more appropriate transition (approximately 3.3-3.8:1 height ratio) while maintaining substantial housing delivery.

**The applicant's own characterisation of scale transition:**

The Design Report (Fieldwork, Part 1, p.17) describes existing Clifton Hill apartment buildings and their relationship to surrounding cottages:

> "They commonly transition dramatically in scale from 3-4 storeys to single storey cottages at street level."

The Planning Report (Tract, p.15) makes the same characterisation in its neighbourhood analysis:

> "They are often 2–4 storeys, larger than the surrounding cottages, creating dramatic scale transitions."

Both the applicant's design team and planning consultants characterise a 2-4 storey to single storey transition as "dramatic." The proposal places an 8 storey building (26.5m) opposite single storey cottages (approximately 6m). The height differential in the proposed condition is more than double the differential the applicant calls dramatic. This is not an external critique; it is the applicant's own analytical framework, applied to the applicant's own proposal. It suggests both the design team and planning consultants recognised the scale sensitivity of the Clifton Hill context but did not carry that recognition through to the proposed building height.

### Finding 4: Planning Report Errors

The Planning Report submitted by Tract Consultants contains six factual errors that understate departures from planning standards. Four relate to matters central to the proposal (building height, zone height limit, decision guideline, setback). Two additional errors (vehicle access road naming and landscaping table row citation) demonstrate carelessness.

#### 3.1: Factual Errors in the Planning Report

The Planning Report contains six factual errors. Four relate to matters central to the proposal (building height, zone height limit, decision guideline, setback) and all understate the degree to which the proposal departs from standards. Two additional errors (vehicle access road naming and landscaping table row citation) are of different character but contribute to a broader pattern of imprecision and consistently citing less stringent provisions than those that actually apply. Beyond these six errors, the Planning Report contains a pattern of imprecision in supporting compliance claims. The tree retention figure is inflated by counting offsite council street trees as on-site retention (pp. 41 and 68). Alexandra Parade East is characterised differently depending on which argument it supports (p. 11 vs p. 48). These are not individually decisive, but they establish that the report's self-assessment of compliance cannot be accepted uncritically.

**Error 1: Building height: 17.57m claimed, actual 26.5m**

The report states (p. 41, section 6.2.2):

> "The maximum building height of 17.57m exceeds this preferred maximum height identified in the GRZ"

This appears in the section specifically discussing GRZ2 height compliance. The actual maximum building height is **26.5 metres** (28.65m with rooftop facilities), as stated in the project summary table in the same document (p. 16) and in the architectural drawings (TP3000 & TP3001).

**The 17.57m figure is incorrect.** It is not identified or explained in the report, does not match the actual building height of 26.5m stated elsewhere in the same document (p. 16), and does not correspond to the podium height (14.3m per elevation drawings) or other documented building elements. The claim understates the building height by nearly 9 metres in the section specifically addressing the zone height limit.

**Error 2: GRZ2 height limit, claimed to be 11m default, actual 9m mandatory**

This error appears twice in the Planning Report, with different wording, creating an internal contradiction.

On page 28 (section 5.2, Zoning Compliance), the report quotes the GRZ2 schedule correctly, then immediately introduces the 11-metre fallback as if it applies:

> "Schedule 2 to the General Residential Zone states that a building used for a dwelling, small second dwelling or residential building must not exceed the height of 9 metres... Pursuant to Clause 32.08-11 [Maximum building height requirement for a dwelling or residential building] a building must not be constructed for use as a dwelling that exceeds 11 metres in height or exceeds three storeys at any point."

On page 41 (section 6.2.2, Planning Assessment), the report flatly denies the schedule provision exists:

> "No maximum building height is specified in Schedule 2 of the General Residential Zone and therefore this proposal is subject to a discretionary maximum height of 11m (3 storeys)"

Both are incorrect. GRZ Schedule 2 clearly states:

> "A building used as a dwelling, small second dwelling or residential building must not exceed the height of **9 metres**."

Clause 32.08-11 (Maximum building height requirement for a dwelling, small second dwelling or residential building) provides an 11-metre fallback only where a schedule does NOT specify a height limit. GRZ2 does specify a 9-metre limit, so the 11-metre fallback does not apply.

The page 28 treatment acknowledges the 9-metre provision but then introduces the 11-metre fallback as though it operates alongside or instead of the schedule. The page 41 treatment contradicts page 28 by claiming no height is specified at all. This internal contradiction indicates a fundamental misreading of how zone schedules operate. The page 41 treatment goes further by denying the schedule provision exists. Both are incorrect, and the effect is to apply the more permissive 11m standard.

The actual zone height control is 9 metres mandatory, not 11 metres discretionary. This makes the exceedance 2.94x instead of 2.4x.

**Error 3: Decision guideline on visual obtrusiveness not addressed**

The GRZ Schedule 2 decision guidelines explicitly ask:

> "Whether opportunities exist to avoid a building being visually obtrusive through the use of alternative building designs, particularly for developments with overall building heights in excess of 9 metres."

Under Clause 52.20-5, the applicant must address the zone's decision guidelines (this is a procedural obligation to address the guideline, not a requirement to comply with it substantively). The Planning Report does not address this guideline, the most directly relevant GRZ2 guideline for an 8 storey proposal. At 26.5m (2.9x the mandatory 9m limit), this guideline is clearly engaged. The guideline specifically asks about "alternative building designs" and a 6-7 storey building (20.1-23.3m) is precisely such an alternative that would better avoid visual obtrusiveness.

**Error 4: Rutland Street setback, claimed minimum 3.45m, actual 2.555m or 3.445m depending on interpretation**

The report claims a minimum Rutland Street setback of 3.45m at ground floor in two places:

- Page 16 (setbacks table): "Podium setback of 3.445m for the southern component up to Level 1"
- Page 49 (Clause 52.20-6.2 assessment): "The proposed building incorporates a minimum setback from the title boundary to Rutland Street of 3.45m at ground floor and Level 1"

The architectural drawings (ground floor plan TP2100, Design Package p. 17) show the main building wall at 3.445m from the Rutland Street boundary, with an uncovered raised platform projecting to 2.555m from the boundary. The architect labels this element "BALCONY 002" (23 sqm), though it is at ground level, which is inconsistent with the ordinary meaning of "balcony" (an elevated platform on an upper floor).

Whether this uncovered ground-level element counts toward the setback measurement depends on its characterisation. Clause 52.20-6.2 permits encroachment by "porches, pergolas and verandahs that are less than 3.6 metres high and eaves." These terms conventionally require roofs. The element in question is uncovered (if it were covered, Level 1 would also be at 2.555m, but the Planning Report claims Level 1 is at 3.445m). An uncovered raised platform at ground level is not listed among the permitted encroachment types in Clause 52.20-6.2.

The minimum setback is either 2.555m (if the uncovered platform is treated as part of the building envelope) or 3.445m (if it is somehow excluded from the measurement). Either way, there is a shortfall against the 4.0m standard: 3.445m represents a 0.555m (14%) shortfall; 2.555m represents a 1.445m (36%) shortfall. For an 8 storey building opposite single storey houses, the Clause 52.20 standard of 4.0m is itself modest; failing to achieve even that minimum is significant regardless of which measurement is adopted.

The same 3.45m figure appears in two separate sections of the report (pp. 16 and 49), both claiming this as the "minimum setback." The report either ignores the projecting ground-floor element entirely or treats it as a permitted encroachment without addressing whether an uncovered raised platform qualifies under the clause's encroachment provisions.

**Error 5: Vehicle access road, claimed "Alexandra Parade", actual Alexandra Parade East**

The report states (p. 7) "vehicle access from Alexandra Parade." This is incorrect. Vehicle access is from **Alexandra Parade East**, a separate 10-metre TRZ2 service road with no direct connection to Alexandra Parade at this location. The specialist Traffic Engineering Assessment correctly identifies "Alexandra Parade E" throughout. This is a drafting error that implies direct arterial access when actual access requires travel via local residential streets.

**Error 6: Landscaping table row, cited "1501-2500 square metres", actual "2501 square metres or more"**

The Planning Report (p. 67) cites the wrong row of Table 5 for the 3,247 sqm site. The proposal complies with the correct, more stringent standard. This is a table lookup error with no compliance consequence.

**Pattern:**

Errors 1–4 all relate to the most significant planning issues (height and street setback), and all have the effect of making the proposal look more compliant than it is. Errors 5 and 6 are of different character (naming inaccuracies and table lookup mistakes rather than compliance understatements) but contribute to a pattern of carelessness and imprecision in the report. Six errors in a single planning report, with the substantive ones (1–4) all understating planning departures (including overstating the Rutland Street minimum setback by ignoring or mischaracterising a ground-floor element projecting to 2.555m), raises legitimate questions about the rigour and reliability of the document. This pattern of internal contradictions extends beyond the numbered errors into the broader compliance assessment: the Alexandra Parade East setback is stated as 0.64m on page 49 (used to justify acceptability) but as 490mm on page 16, with drawings consistent with the lower figure.

The Planning Report also contradicts itself on the western boundary relationship. Page 11 correctly describes "The VicTrack owned laneway of approximately 4m in width, which abuts the railway line further west" with the site boundary being a chain link fence between the site and the laneway. Two pages later (p. 13), the report states "The site abuts the Hurstbridge/Mernda railway line to the west," which is factually incorrect. While the technical assessments (Design Report's AS5100 analysis, Acoustic Report noise measurements) correctly account for the laneway's width and measure from actual site boundaries (though AS5100 is not obviously applicable at all, and the Design Report mischaracterises AS5100's tiered requirements; see Modification 3 discussion), the internal contradiction further illustrates careless drafting. The 4m laneway is VicTrack access land (not part of the Metro Trains Melbourne lease) and provides separation relevant to the AS5100 clearance zone, noise buffering, and western interface amenity.

**Assessment:**

Under Clause 52.20-5 (Requirements for plans and documents), the applicant must adequately address zone purposes, decision guidelines, and all relevant provisions. When the Planning Report misstates the building height by 9 metres (Error 1), incorrectly claims no schedule height limit exists (Error 2), omits the visual obtrusiveness guideline (Error 3), and overstates the Rutland Street minimum setback (Error 4), the Clause 52.20-5 obligation has not been adequately discharged.

### Finding 5: Crossover/Underpass Safety Conflict Not Assessed

**The standard:**

Clause 52.20-7.18 (Access) requires that "The location of crossovers should maximise pedestrian safety and the provision of on-street visitor car parking and on-street bicycle lanes."

**The conflict:**

The applicant's documents show a pedestrian and cycling underpass on Alexandra Parade East with its exit directly opposite the proposed vehicle crossover:

- Traffic Engineering Assessment, pp. 7, 17 (site context maps showing underpass location)
- Design Report Part 1, p. 14 ("Green Travel Infrastructure" map identifying the underpass)

The City of Yarra's "Safer Streets Clifton Hill and North Abbotsford" consultation identifies Alexandra Parade East as part of a safe alternate cycling route in the area.

**The omission:**

Neither the Traffic Engineering Assessment nor the Planning Report mentions or assesses this interaction. The Traffic Engineering Assessment contains no mention of the underpass in its cycling infrastructure section (p. 10) or walking infrastructure section (p. 11). The Planning Report's Clause 52.20-7.18 compliance statement (p. 78, Appendix A) addresses only pedestrian access to the building from Rutland and Noone Streets, not the impact of the crossover location on existing public pedestrian and cyclist movements.

Additionally, the Traffic Engineering Assessment is missing key data, with appendices B (Car Parking Survey Results), C (Car Parking and Access Arrangements Review), and D (Swept Path Diagrams) being empty apart from title pages.

**Assessment:**

Locating a vehicle crossover generating approximately 105 daily movements (Traffic Engineering Assessment, p. 24: 35 spaces with standard residential trip generation) directly opposite a pedestrian and cyclist arrival point, without any assessment of the resulting conflict, does not "maximise pedestrian safety." The applicant identified the infrastructure on their own site context map, then failed to assess whether the crossover location complies with the standard.

This is not a variation formally flagged by the Planning Report. It is an omission. The crossover location has not been assessed against the explicit pedestrian safety standard in Clause 52.20-7.18 (Access), preventing the Minister from determining whether the standard is satisfied.

Relocating vehicle access to Noone Street (Modification 2, discussed in Part 3) would resolve this compliance gap by eliminating the crossover/underpass conflict entirely.

### Finding 6: Missing Wind Assessment

**The requirement:**

Clause 52.20-7.21 (Wind impacts) requires that "Buildings of 5 or more storeys should not cause unsafe wind conditions in publicly accessible areas on or off the site."

**The compliance claim:**

The Planning Report references wind advice from Windtech Consultants (pp. 7, 27) and claims compliance with Clause 52.20-7.21 in Appendix A (p. 80). The report states (p. 27) that the design "included several design features and wind mitigating strategies to ensure the building will be suitable for the intended use within outdoor trafficable areas" and (p. 80) that "the development will not create unacceptable wind conditions and will achieve the relevant comfort criterion." The report provides no supporting analysis or data.

**The problem:**

The wind assessment report was not provided during the community design consultation period and is not among the submitted documents. For an 8 storey building in a low-rise residential area, ground-level wind effects at the Rutland Street and Noone Street interfaces are legitimate amenity concerns. The absence of the Windtech report makes compliance with Clause 52.20-7.21 (Wind impacts) unverifiable.

**Procedural significance:**

The Clause 52.20-4 (Community consultation) community consultation is the sole mechanism for public participation in a process with no VCAT review. The wind assessment's absence during this consultation period (after the design package was released) prevented community members from making informed submissions on wind impacts.

Community members could not:
- Assess whether "suitable for the intended use within outdoor trafficable areas" and meeting "the relevant comfort criterion" are appropriate standards for a residential street with single storey houses opposite
- Verify whether the assessment modelled the correct building configuration
- Evaluate what assumptions were made about surrounding context
- Determine whether the assessment considered impacts on the Rutland Street interface

Even if the wind assessment is supplied with the formal application, the consultation process has already concluded without this information. The applicant asserts compliance with a specific development standard, relies on technical evidence to support that assertion, but does not provide that evidence for public scrutiny.

## Part 3: The Path Forward

### Proposed Modifications

This analysis supports redevelopment of this site for social housing. The existing 30 walk-up units don't meet contemporary standards, the site is well-suited to higher density, and there's a clear need for more social housing in inner Melbourne. The question isn't whether to redevelop, but at what scale and with what design accommodations.

A new build is strongly preferred over renovation as it can deliver substantially more housing (86-108 dwellings vs 30) and achieve contemporary standards in accessibility, energy efficiency, and liveability that renovation cannot.

The following modifications would retain these benefits while addressing the most significant departures from planning controls and genuine community safety concerns. The modifications are structured in two tiers: Tier 1 modifications are achievable within the existing building footprint; Tier 2 modifications require westward building adjustment likely subject to AS5100 risk assessment. Each modification improves the viability of the others.

#### Modification 1: Reduce Height to 6 Storeys

**Why 6 storeys:**

The Planning Report cites three nearby "emerging medium-rise infill" developments as evidence of neighbourhood character evolution:

- 210 Alexandra Parade East: 6 storeys
- 122 Roseneath Street: 6 storeys
- Clifton House: 4 storeys

These are the tallest buildings in the suburb. A 6 storey building (18.6m) matches the local precedent the applicant cited.

**Yield comparison:**

| Height | Estimated dwellings | Multiplier vs existing 30 | Net vs proposed 114 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 storeys (HV proposal) | 114 | 3.8x | - |
| 7 storeys | ~100-108 | 3.3-3.6x | -6 to -14 |
| 6 storeys (HV footprint) | 86-94 | 2.9-3.1x | -20 to -28 |
| 6 storeys (with westward shift) | 95-105 | 3.2-3.5x | -9 to -19 |

A 6 storey building with westward adjustment delivers 95-105 dwellings (83-92% of the proposed yield) with substantially better planning policy alignment. Even without westward shift, 6 storeys delivers 86-94 dwellings (75-82% of the proposed yield). Both represent substantial increases over the existing 30 units (2.9-3.5x multiplier).

**Planning benefits of 6 storeys:**

- **Still exceeds the 9-metre zone limit** but materially changes the scale relationship with the neighbourhood:
  - 6 storeys: 18.6m = 2.07x override (matches Mount Waverley, the only other GRZ2 project on the register)
  - 7 storeys: 22.5m = 2.50x override (above the two closest GRZ precedents, both in GRZ3 public housing schedules)
  - 8 storeys: 26.5m = 2.94x override (42% above next closest GRZ precedent)
- Matches the two tallest buildings in Clifton Hill (210 Alexandra Parade East and 122 Roseneath Street), the established maximum built form in the suburb
- Directly addresses the GRZ2 decision guideline about visual obtrusiveness for buildings over 9 metres (offers "alternative building design")
- Consistent with the applicant's own characterisation of Clifton Hill's scale transitions, which describes 2-4 storeys to single storey as "dramatic" (see Finding 2)
- Appropriate transition to single storey houses opposite (6 storeys creates approximately 3.3:1 height ratio vs 8 storeys at 4.4:1)
- Reduced overshadowing and overlooking impacts
- Better alignment with incremental change area policy

**Proportionality under Clause 71.02-3:**

The [integrated decision-making framework](https://www.besthooper.com.au/Insights-library/integrated-decision-making-a-timely-reminder/) at Clause 71.02-3 (Integrated decision making) requires the Minister to balance competing objectives in favour of net community benefit. The critical question is proportionality: do the last two storeys (Levels 7-8), delivering 9-19 additional dwellings, justify:

- The largest GRZ height override in the Clause 52.20 register (2.94x, 42% above next closest at 2.46x, which is in GRZ3, a schedule designated for public housing sites)
- Conflict with multiple planning policies (incremental change classification, MPS mid-rise direction, Rutland Street interface)
- A 4.4:1 height ratio to single storey houses across one residential street
- Exceeding the tallest existing buildings in the neighbourhood

These 9-19 dwellings represent real housing need. However, this is one of many projects to improve social housing in inner Melbourne. Homes Victoria delivers social housing across Victoria, including projects within or near zone height limits. The question is not whether these dwellings should be built, but whether this specific site's planning context justifies the policy cost of building them here at 8 storeys rather than 6.

At 6 storeys delivering 95-105 dwellings, the development still more than triples the existing housing stock (3.2-3.5x) while achieving materially better policy alignment. This is the integrated decision-making calculus the Minister must undertake.

**Funding question:** Submitters may wish to ask whether the Commonwealth Housing Australia Future Fund (HAFF) funding for this project is contingent on delivering 114 dwellings specifically, or whether it would fund the project at a modified yield of 86-108 dwellings. This information is relevant to assessing the viability implications of height reduction.

#### Modification 2: Relocate Vehicle Access to Noone Street

**Planning merit:**

Relocating vehicle access from Alexandra Parade East to Noone Street delivers better planning outcomes under Clause 71.02-3 (Integrated decision making). The current access arrangement creates three planning concerns that Noone Street access would resolve.

**The current access problem:**

The Homes Victoria proposal takes all vehicle access from Alexandra Parade East. The Traffic Engineering Assessment estimates approximately 105 daily vehicle movements (35 spaces with typical residential trip generation).

**Alexandra Parade East is zoned TRZ2 but functions as a local access road.** Despite its Transport Zone 2 classification, it has no vehicular interchange with the Eastern Freeway at this location. The freeway runs below grade, separated by sound barriers, pedestrian/cycling path, mature trees, and vertical grade change. The Planning Report itself acknowledges this functional character, describing it as "a local residential street of approximately 10m in width" (p. 11), though it inconsistently characterises it elsewhere as "Transport Zone 2" and "effectively a service lane" (p. 48) when justifying the setback variation.

**All vehicle approaches to Alexandra Parade East require traversing local residential streets:**

- **From south** (via Trennery Crescent): Noone Street → Rutland Street → Alexandra Parade East → site entrance
- **From east** (via Hoddle Street southbound): Noone or Roseneath Street → Rutland Street → Alexandra Parade East → site entrance
- **From east** (via Hoddle Street northbound): Roseneath Street only (no right turn from Hoddle into Noone) → Rutland Street → Alexandra Parade East → site entrance

Note: Trennery Crescent passes under the Eastern Freeway without access to it. To reach the freeway from the site, vehicles must exit onto Alexandra Parade East, turn left into Rutland, left into Noone, left onto Hoddle, then left via slip road to access Alexandra Parade proper.

There is no direct approach that avoids Rutland Street, and either Noone or Roseneath Street. The current design concentrates all 105 daily vehicle movements through residential streets to reach a narrow service road with an existing pedestrian/cyclist safety concern at the underpass.

**The underpass conflict and Clause 52.20-7.18 compliance failure:**

Alexandra Parade East at this location has a pedestrian and cycling underpass that connects under the Eastern Freeway, running from Alexandra Parade East beneath the railway line towards Hoddle Street. The underpass exit on Alexandra Parade East is located directly opposite the proposed vehicle crossover. The applicant's own Design Report Part 1 (p. 14, "Green Travel Infrastructure" map) identifies this underpass as existing active pedestrian and cyclist infrastructure. The City of Yarra's "Safer Streets Clifton Hill and North Abbotsford" consultation identifies Alexandra Parade East as part of a safe alternate cycling route in the area.

Despite mapping this infrastructure themselves, neither the Traffic Engineering Assessment (Traffix Group, January 2026) nor the Planning Report (Tract, January 2026) acknowledges or assesses the interaction between the proposed vehicle crossover and the underpass exit. The Traffic Engineering Assessment (25 pages) does not mention the underpass anywhere in its cycling infrastructure analysis (p. 10) or walking infrastructure analysis (p. 11). The Planning Report's Clause 52.20-7.18 compliance statement (p. 78) addresses only pedestrian access to the building from Rutland and Noone Streets. It does not address the impact of the crossover location on existing public pedestrian and cyclist movements on Alexandra Parade East.

**This is a compliance failure.** Clause 52.20-7.18 (Access) states:

> Pedestrian and cyclist access should be clearly delineated from vehicle access.
>
> The location of crossovers should maximise pedestrian safety and the provision of on-street visitor car parking and on-street bicycle lanes.

Locating a vehicle crossover generating approximately 105 daily movements (Traffic Engineering Assessment p. 18: 35 spaces with standard residential trip generation) directly opposite a known pedestrian and cyclist arrival point, without any assessment of the resulting conflict, does not "maximise pedestrian safety." The applicant identified the infrastructure on their own site context map, then failed to assess whether the crossover location complies with the explicit pedestrian safety standard in Clause 52.20-7.18 (Access).

The Traffic Engineering Assessment does not quantify existing pedestrian or cyclist usage of the underpass. The underpass is in the southwest corner of Clifton Hill where the freeway blocks many pedestrian and cycle routes that would otherwise head south, giving it decent usage despite not being a primary connection. The applicant's failure to measure and report this usage prevents proper assessment of the conflict magnitude, but the structural problem is clear: concentrating vehicle entries at a pedestrian/cyclist egress point creates safety risks that a Noone Street access would avoid entirely.

**Ground-level car park trades planning outcomes for cost efficiency:**

The Planning Report justifies the ground-level open car park over a basement on grounds of cost. Page 22 cites "cost-efficiencies" alongside Crime Prevention Through Environmental Design (CPTED) benefits. Page 55 states that basement car parking would "introduce significant additional cost that would be inconsistent with the objectives of the project and Clause 52.20 to deliver high-quality new social housing at scale." The Planning Report also claims ground-level parking provides "natural light, open-air design, clear sightlines for passive surveillance, and reduced hidden areas." The CPTED claim does not withstand scrutiny. Passive surveillance requires people positioned to observe activity. The car park faces a railway embankment (west) and quiet laneway (south) with no pedestrian presence. The development's ground-floor apartments face Rutland Street and Noone Street, not the car park. Upper-level apartments have only oblique downward views. The car park does not achieve "natural surveillance and pedestrian visibility from adjacent buildings" as required by Clause 52.20-6.7 (Car parking).

The ground-level car park has planning consequences. It consumes space that could provide landscaping, deep soil planting, and permeability (the site achieves 17.6% against the 20% standard). It positions parking, bins, and services on the western ground level, limiting landscape integration. Most significantly, it locks in the Alexandra Parade East vehicle access configuration. A basement car park would free the western ground level for alternative vehicle access from Noone Street via the western boundary or the existing 9.555m northern gap. Cost efficiency is a legitimate project consideration, but it is not a planning merit test under Clause 52.20. The Minister should scrutinise cases where cost-driven design choices are subsequently described as amenity benefits.

**Preferred: Noone Street direct entry**

Vehicle access directly from Noone Street would:
- **Achieve Clause 52.20-7.18 (Access) compliance** - eliminates the crossover/underpass conflict, properly satisfying the "maximise pedestrian safety" standard that the current design fails to address
- **Improve pedestrian and cyclist safety** - removes conflicts at Alexandra Parade East underpass exit point where cyclists and pedestrians emerge directly opposite the proposed vehicle crossover
- **Enhance Rutland Street interface amenity** - removes all 105 daily development-generated vehicle movements from Rutland Street (the residential frontage where single storey Victorian terraces sit directly opposite)
- **Use appropriate traffic distribution** - Noone Street has residential street character suitable for local traffic, avoiding the circuitous three-turn path through residential streets required for the current Alexandra Parade East access
- **Simplify access** - provides one-turn access for vehicles approaching from north or west
- **No third-party coordination** - uses only land within the site and public road, no VicTrack negotiation or council approvals beyond the planning decision

**Implementation: The modification can be achieved via the existing western gap or improved with westward building adjustment**

The existing design has a 9.555m northern gap from the western boundary, sufficient to accommodate vehicle access from Noone Street without any building shift. A standard 3.0-3.5m driveway fits within this space, with 6-6.5m remaining for landscaping. Requires reconfiguring internal carpark circulation (currently designed for southern entry) and relocating the railway garden to the podium or Rutland frontage.

Westward building adjustment (Modification 3) would improve vehicle circulation geometry and enable additional benefits (generous Rutland setback, enhanced landscaping), but is not required for vehicle access relocation itself. Three approaches are detailed in Modification 3, ranging from using the existing gap (no shift) to substantial westward shift (6-9m) for maximum Rutland setback and landscape quality.

**Benefits common to all approaches:**

1. **Removes traffic from Alexandra Parade East underpass conflict zone** - pedestrian/cyclist safety improvement
2. **Removes traffic from Rutland Street Rutland Street interface** - no vehicle crossings on the sensitive eastern frontage
3. **Eliminates three-turn circulation through residential streets** - simple one-turn entry from Noone Street
4. **Volume is manageable** - 35 spaces generating ~105 movements/day on a residential street is within normal parameters
5. **Uses only land within the site** - no VicTrack negotiation, no council approval required beyond the planning decision

**Compliance and implementation:** The modification maintains compliance with Clause 52.20-6.7 (Car parking) standards (forward exit, passing areas, sight lines, gradients, landscaping). The 9.555m western gap accommodates standard driveway width with space for WSUD treatment and landscaping. Internal carpark circulation requires redesign for northern entry (currently configured for southern entry). The Waste Management Plan's on-site collection remains compatible with either street frontage.

Detailed traffic engineering review is needed to confirm Noone Street's capacity, sight lines, and that 35 spaces remain achievable with reconfigured layout.

#### Modification 3: Westward Building Adjustment (Optional Enhancement)

**The available space:**

The Homes Victoria design has two different western setbacks from the lot boundary (railway side):
- **Northern podium section: 9.555m** (provides more generous space)
- **Southern main building: 8.086m** (closest point to railway)

This space is currently used for the railway garden landscape element and provides AS5100 clearance. The 8.086m southern setback determines the AS5100 compliance position, as it is the closest point to the railway.

**AS5100 clearance requirements and applicability:**

AS5100.1:2017 is a bridge design standard. Its scope (Section 1.1) states it applies to "road, rail, pedestrian and bicycle-path bridges, and other associated structures." Section 15.3.5 establishes tiered requirements for structures adjacent to railway corridors, measured from the "centre-line of the nearest track":

- **Beyond 20m:** No derailment protection required (Section 15.3.5: "need not be designed for derailment collision protection").
- **10-20m:** Risk assessment required, subject to approval of the relevant authorities, **and** design for the collision loads specified in Clause 11.4.2.4 of AS 5100.2.
- **Within 10m:** Design for the collision loads specified in Clause 11.4.2.3 of AS 5100.2 (a more onerous standard than Clause 11.4.2.4).

**Whether AS5100 applies to residential buildings is uncertain.** The standard's scope (Section 1.1) covers "road, rail, pedestrian and bicycle-path bridges, and other associated structures." Section 15.3.5, which establishes the tiered clearance requirements the Design Report appears to reference, is specifically titled "Abutments" and deals with bridge abutments adjacent to railway corridors, not residential buildings on private land. Metro Trains Melbourne's mandatory engineering standard L1-CHE-STD-040 (Bridge Standard) references AS5100 but limits application to "Bridges, Underpasses, Soil Retaining Structures, Culverts and associated civil infrastructure within the rail corridor or the MTM Infrastructure Lease," with no provisions for residential buildings on adjacent private land. No Victorian building regulation, NCC provision, or mandatory standard requires residential buildings adjacent to rail corridors to comply with AS5100 derailment protection provisions.

**Residential buildings adjacent to rail corridors are commonplace throughout Melbourne, including at least one large-scale multi-storey development in the City of Yarra.** The Hub at 8 Garfield Street, Richmond (9 storeys, 170 apartments, completed 2015, Yarra City Council) was built immediately adjacent to an active rail embankment with no laneway gap (closer than the lot boundaries at this site allow), demonstrating that recent large-scale multi-storey residential development immediately adjacent to active rail is both structurally feasible and has received planning approval (noting The Hub is in Mixed Use Zone in an area with denser development context including many 3-4 storey buildings). One of the existing multi-storey 1950s walk-up buildings on the proposed site sits well inside the 20m zone, built close to the western lot boundary, and has operated for ~70 years, demonstrating that multi-storey residential use at this proximity to the stabling sidings is feasible. Additionally, 110 Noone Street (two-storey residential, built 1980-1990, immediately north of the proposed site) sits approximately 10m from an active commuter line, facing demonstrably higher derailment risk than the proposed site (active commuter line with frequent movements at line speed vs low-speed stabling siding). Many nearby residential properties operate within 20m of non-siding lines.

The architectural drawings show the 20m clearance measured from "structure zone to centre of stabling shed". No physical shed structure exists at this location; this is the Victoria Park sidings area where trains are stabled on the tracks. The Design Report Part 2 (Fieldwork, p. 55, perspective view point 4) clarifies that "the western edge of the building is set back 20m from the centre of the nearest railway line", confirming the measurement is from the railway line centreline, not a physical shed. The western lot boundary is **11.92m from this reference point** (measured from the scale drawings). AS5100.1:2017 Section 15.3.5 requires measurement from "centre-line of the nearest track", which is consistent with the Design Report's stated measurement basis.

The proposed building, positioned with an 8.086m setback from the western boundary, sits at approximately 20m from the railway reference point (at the threshold between the "risk assessment and design" zone and the "no protection needed" zone).

**Should the building be shifted west?**

This modification is **optional** and **subject to assessment of structural requirements for building adjacent to rail**. The benefit is improved Rutland Street setback and enhanced landscaping opportunities. The potential cost (if structural requirements apply) would be determined through assessment.

**Likely mitigating factors for assessment scope and structural cost:** The measurement is from a stabling siding (low-speed operations) with parked trains creating a physical barrier. The lot boundary at 11.92m is only 1.92m inside the 20m threshold. The existing buildings on site have operated within this zone for 70 years. Whether AS5100 applies to residential buildings is uncertain given the standard's scope (bridge abutments, not residential buildings) and the absence of provisions in MTM's mandatory engineering standard L1-CHE-STD-040. Even if structural requirements were to apply, they would need to be determined through proper assessment appropriate to the actual risk profile (stabling siding context, low-speed operations, distance from tracks).

**The Design Report's treatment of AS5100 appears to have justified an unnecessarily conservative building position.** Design Report Part 1 (Fieldwork, p. 32) states that AS5100 "defines increased structural requirements for any building structure to withstand impact from potential train derailment" and that "[b]uildings proposed within this 20m zone typically incur a significant cost uplift to meet all associated structural requirements." Page 35 states the 20m setback is maintained "to avoid triggering AS5100 requirements, which mandate enhanced structural measures to withstand potential train derailment impacts."

Even if AS5100 were to apply to residential buildings (which is uncertain given the standard's scope and the absence of provisions in MTM's L1-CHE-STD-040), this treatment conflates two distinct zones with materially different requirements. The 0-10m zone requires design for Clause 11.4.2.3 collision loads (the full derailment impact standard). The 10-20m zone requires risk assessment and design for Clause 11.4.2.4 collision loads, a graduated standard that reflects reduced risk at greater distance. By treating the entire 0-20m distance as uniformly triggering "mandated enhanced structural measures" with "significant cost uplift", the Design Report overstates the constraint for the 10-20m zone where different, less onerous requirements apply.

**This treatment has material design consequences.** The applicant has not provided an AS5100 risk assessment (or assessment of actual structural requirements for building adjacent to rail) with the planning application. The current design positions the building at approximately 20m from the railway reference line, avoiding the 10-20m zone entirely. By not conducting an assessment, the applicant has foregone the opportunity to determine whether shifting the building west into the 10-20m zone is structurally and economically viable. Such a shift would enable generous Rutland Street setbacks, enhanced landscaping, and better Rutland Street interface outcomes. The western lot boundary is 11.92m from the railway reference point: only 1.92m past the 20m threshold, and 8.08m clear of the 10m boundary where more onerous requirements would apply. At this favourable position (near the low-risk end of the 10-20m zone, adjacent to a low-speed stabling siding, with the existing buildings demonstrating 70 years of accepted proximity), the structural requirements and associated costs should have been investigated during design development, not assumed to be prohibitive.

**The failure to commission this assessment during design development was an error.** Determining whether westward positioning could achieve better planning outcomes should have been part of proper design due diligence. Instead, the design was constrained by avoiding assessment entirely, resulting in suboptimal Rutland Street interface, substandard setbacks, and constrained landscaping. The existing buildings on site operated successfully within the 20m zone for 70 years, demonstrating that residential use at this proximity to the stabling sidings is feasible.

**Three westward adjustment options:**

**Option A: Minimal shift (2-4m) - Improved setback and vehicle circulation**

- Building shifts 2-4m west
- Rutland setback increases from 2.555m (balcony face) to 4.5-6.5m at ground floor
- Cleaner Noone Street driveway geometry (supports Modification 2 Approach B)
- Building position: 16-18m from stabling siding centreline (2-4m into risk zone, approaching "no problem" threshold)
- AS5100 risk assessment required; favorable position for minimal strengthening conclusion
- Estimated yield: ~100-105 dwellings at 7 storeys, ~95-100 at 6 storeys

**Option B: Moderate shift (4-6m) - Generous setback**

- Building shifts 4-6m west
- Rutland setback increases to 6.5-8.5m
- Enables canopy tree planting and substantial front gardens
- Building position: 14-16m from stabling siding centreline (well into risk zone but favourable context)
- Estimated yield: ~98-105 dwellings at 7 storeys, ~92-98 at 6 storeys

**Option C: Substantial shift (6-9m) - Maximum green space**

- Building shifts 6-9m west (approaching or at lot boundary)
- Rutland setback increases to 8-12m (generous garden precinct)
- Building position: 11.92-14m from stabling siding centreline
- Can potentially widen floor plate to offset height reduction yield loss
- Estimated yield: ~100-108 dwellings at 7 storeys, ~95-105 at 6 storeys (if floor plate widens)

**Yield estimate methodology:** A 2-6m westward shift increases building east-west width by 2-6m. At a building length of approximately 40m and 6-7 storeys, additional width provides 80-240m² per floor or 480-1,680m² total GFA. At typical dwelling sizes of 50-80m² (mix of 1/2/3-bed units), this enables approximately 6-21 additional dwellings depending on shift magnitude and design efficiency. **Important caveat: These yield estimates are speculative.** They are subject to detailed architectural design including Clause 52.20-7.16 (Natural ventilation, 40% cross-ventilation requirement, which the current design barely meets at 39.5%), 52.20-7.4 (Daylight), and apartment mix requirements. Wider floor plates can compromise cross-ventilation performance for single-aspect units, potentially reducing achievable yields below these estimates. We cannot produce detailed floor plans, that is the applicant's responsibility. These figures illustrate potential order of magnitude for planning assessment purposes, not confirmed achievable yield.

**Benefits of westward adjustment:**

1. **Improved Rutland Street setback** - 6-12m enables genuine front gardens, canopy trees, and enhanced streetscape
2. **Enhanced vehicle circulation** - cleaner Noone Street driveway alignment (supports Modification 2)
3. **Reduced overshadowing** - building moves away from eastern Rutland Street interface
4. **Better permeability** - additional deep soil area in increased setback zone
5. **Potential yield optimization** - wider floor plate may offset dwelling loss from height reduction

**This modification is subject to assessment of structural requirements for building adjacent to rail** (whether under AS5100 if deemed applicable, or through VicTrack Rail Development Interface Guidelines risk assessment) to determine requirements at the proposed building position. The applicant has not provided this assessment with the application. The current design avoids the 10-20m zone by positioning the building at approximately 20m from the railway reference line. This conservative approach may have unnecessarily constrained design options. Even if AS5100 were deemed applicable (despite uncertainty about its scope extending to residential buildings), the 10-20m zone requires risk assessment and graduated collision load design (Clause 11.4.2.4), not the full collision load design (Clause 11.4.2.3) required within 10m. Conducting assessment during design development, rather than avoiding it, could have revealed whether westward positioning is structurally and economically feasible at this favourable location (1.92m past the 20m threshold, stabling siding context, well clear of the 10m boundary, with existing buildings demonstrating 70 years of accepted proximity). This would have enabled the applicant to design with generous Rutland setbacks and enhanced landscaping from the outset rather than requiring post-approval modifications.

#### Modification 4: Increase Rutland Street Setback to 4.0m+ at Ground Floor

**The current shortfall:**

The ground floor Rutland Street setback is either 2.555m (to uncovered raised platform) or 3.445m (to main building wall) against a 4.0m Clause 52.20-6.2 (Buildings and works) standard. The architect labels the ground-floor element "BALCONY 002" though it is at ground level and uncovered. Whether an uncovered raised platform qualifies as a permitted encroachment under Clause 52.20-6.2 (which lists "porches, pergolas and verandahs that are less than 3.6 metres high and eaves," conventionally requiring roofs) is ambiguous. Either measurement falls short of the 4.0m standard: 3.445m is 0.555m (14%) short; 2.555m is 1.445m (36%) short. For an 8 storey building opposite heritage houses, failing to achieve the 4.0m standard is significant regardless of which measurement applies.

The shortfall is compounded by a road reserve encroachment issue. The Planning Report (p. 9) acknowledges that existing fence lines along Rutland Street extend approximately 1.9m into the public road reserve (approximately 117 sqm of public land). The landscape plans show front gardens extending into this public land. When the encroachment is corrected as part of the redevelopment, the actual setback becomes the experienced distance from the street, and the landscape treatment shown in the design documents cannot be delivered as designed within the title boundary. Achieving the 4.0m minimum standard (or greater with westward adjustment) provides adequate space within the site for the landscape strategy the design documents intend.

**Proposed modification:**

Increase the ground floor setback to 4.0m minimum (achieving standard compliance), or greater if westward building adjustment (Modification 3) is approved. Level 1+ already provides 4.76m (exceeding the standard), so only ground floor requires modification.

**Two scenarios:**

**Without westward adjustment:** Increase ground floor setback to 4.0m by moving the building face west within the existing footprint. The extent of adjustment needed (0.555m if measuring from main wall, or 1.445m if measuring from raised platform) depends on how the ground-floor element is characterised, but in either case the building must be redesigned to achieve the 4.0m standard. This reduces building width, requiring some internal layout adjustment to maintain yield, but achieves standard compliance.

**With westward adjustment (Modification 3):** The entire building shifts west, enabling Rutland setback of 6-12m (depending on Option A/B/C) while maintaining or increasing building width through use of the 8.086m western gap. This enables yield retention or recovery (potential for wider floor plates) while achieving generous front garden space for substantial landscaping. This approach turns the setback compliance requirement into a dual benefit: improved amenity AND maintained/increased dwelling yield.

**Benefits:**

1. **Eliminates the Rutland Street setback variation** - achieves compliance with Clause 52.20-6.2 (4.0m minimum)
2. **Improves Rutland Street interface** - creates more distance and landscaping buffer to houses opposite
3. **Improves overshadowing** - reduces overshadowing on Rutland Street buildings
4. **Better ground-level landscaping** - 4.0m minimum replaces the current 2.55m constrained strip; 6-12m (with westward adjustment) enables canopy trees and genuine front gardens
5. **Addresses permeability shortfall** - additional pervious surface area in increased setback partially addresses the 2.4 percentage point shortfall (17.6% provided vs 20% required under Clause 52.20-6.3)
6. **Enables genuine on-site canopy coverage** - creates space for substantial canopy trees within site boundaries, addressing the current reliance on offsite council street trees and canopy overhang into road reserve (see Finding 3.2, item 10a)
7. **Streetscape quality** - delivers the "front setbacks affording opportunities for deep soil planting" the Planning Report claims but doesn't currently provide

#### Modification 5: Enhanced Landscaping Strategy for Rutland Street Interface

**Current situation:**

Ground-level landscaping is constrained on all frontages:
- Alexandra Parade East: 0.49m (essentially on-boundary)

### How the Modifications Work Together

The modifications are synergistic and can be implemented in tiers:

**Tier 1 modifications (achievable without building shift):**

1. **Reduce to 6-7 storeys** (Modification 1) - improves Rutland Street interface, addresses zone height override concern, aligns with neighbourhood precedent

2. **Relocate vehicle access to Noone Street via existing western gap** (Modification 2 Approach A) - improves pedestrian safety, removes traffic from residential Rutland Street, eliminates underpass conflicts

3. **Increase Rutland setback to 4.0m minimum** (Modification 4 without westward adjustment) - achieves standard compliance, some landscaping improvement

**Tier 2 modifications (subject to AS5100 assessment before decision):**

4. **Westward building adjustment** (Modification 3) - enables generous Rutland setback (6-12m), cleaner vehicle circulation, enhanced landscaping, potential yield increase through wider floor plate. Requires AS5100 risk assessment. Building measured from stabling siding, 1.92m into risk zone at lot boundary.

5. **Enhanced landscaping with canopy trees** (Modification 5) - uses generous setback space (6-12m) from westward adjustment to deliver substantial urban greening and verifiable on-site canopy coverage within title boundaries

**The tiered structure provides the Minister with flexible options:**

- **Approve Tier 1 modifications** - height reduction + vehicle access via existing gap + minimum setback compliance. These require no structural assessment (though building redesign is necessary to implement the modifications). Roughly triples existing housing.

- **Require AS5100 assessment before deciding on Tier 2** - The applicant has not provided an AS5100.1:2017 Section 15.3.5 risk assessment. The current design positions the building to avoid the 10-20m zone (at approximately 20m from the railway reference line), but this conservative approach may have unnecessarily constrained design options. Within the 10-20m zone, the standard requires both risk assessment (subject to approval of the relevant authorities) and design for Clause 11.4.2.4 collision loads. These are graduated requirements, less onerous than the Clause 11.4.2.3 loads applying within 10m, and their practical cost depends on site-specific factors (stabling siding context, distance from 10m threshold, building design). The assessment during design development could have enabled the applicant to explore westward positions delivering superior planning outcomes. The Minister should require Homes Victoria to complete the AS5100 risk assessment and Clause 11.4.2.4 structural analysis before making a decision. If the assessment confirms the westward shift is feasible without prohibitive structural costs, the Minister can approve with westward adjustment to achieve the generous Rutland setback (6-12m), enhanced landscaping, improved vehicle circulation, and increased yield of Tier 2 modifications. If the assessment identifies significant costs or structural constraints that make the westward shift infeasible, Tier 1 modifications alone deliver a defensible planning outcome.

This pre-decision assessment approach provides the Minister with better information for decision-making: the structural feasibility question is resolved before approval, enabling a more informed decision about whether to approve with Tier 1 modifications alone or with Tier 2 westward adjustment included.

### Precedent Analysis: Detailed Discussion

The [Big Housing Build Register](https://www.planning.vic.gov.au/planning-approvals/big-housing-build-register) lists 59 approved projects as of early 2026. Most are small-scale (1-2 storeys, 2-35 dwellings in regional and outer suburban locations). Eighteen projects are 5 or more storeys.

#### Zone classification methodology

All significant projects (5+ storeys) have been verified via VicPlan, the Victorian Government's interactive planning scheme map. Zone classifications are confirmed by searching each project address in VicPlan and cross-referencing with municipal planning schemes where schedules provide additional detail.

#### Height estimation methodology

Where exact building heights are not publicly disclosed, heights are estimated using the following methods (in order of preference):

1. **Architectural plans**: Measured heights from elevation drawings showing highest natural ground level to building high point (e.g. Mount Waverley: 18.6m from east elevation; Bills Street: 22.5m from north elevation)
2. **Planning reports or register descriptions**: Official height statements from planning documents or the Big Housing Build Register
3. **Project websites and media**: Developer, architect, or builder websites providing specifications (e.g. Shiel Street: 8 storeys per GRC Buxton project page)
4. **Floor count estimation using precedent buildings**: Where only floor count is known, height estimated using measured precedent buildings or floor-to-floor rates derived from them:
   - **Use precedent building heights directly where available:**
     - 6 storeys: 18.6m (Mount Waverley measured precedent)
     - 7 storeys: 22.5m (Bills Street measured precedent)
     - 8 storeys: 26.5m (Clifton Hill proposal and Wellington Street at 26.4m)
   - **For other storey counts, use floor-to-floor rates:**
     - 1-6 storeys: 3.1m per storey (based on 6 storey precedent)
     - 7-8 storeys: 3.2m per storey (based on 7-8 storey precedents)
     - 9-10 storeys: 3.3m per storey (conservative for taller buildings)
     - 11+ storeys: 3.4m per storey (taller buildings typically have higher ceilings)
   - **Note:** Planning height is measured to roof level, not including rooftop plant/services. Actual heights vary with ceiling heights, floor construction depth, site topography, and architectural design. These estimates provide indicative heights for comparison purposes.

**Example calculations:**
- 5 storeys: 5 x 3.1m = 15.5m
- 9 storeys: 9 x 3.3m = 29.7m
- 12 storeys: 12 x 3.4m = 40.8m

Basement levels are counted separately from above-ground storeys and do not contribute to building height calculations for planning assessment purposes.

Sources are documented in the precedent analysis table with preference given to architectural plans and official planning documents over estimates.

## Conclusion

This analysis supports redevelopment for social housing at 6-7 storeys (86-108 dwellings, 2.9-3.6x the existing 30 units) rather than the proposed 8 storeys. The modified scale better aligns with planning policy (incremental change classification, mid-rise location direction, Rutland Street interface) while still roughly tripling or more than tripling housing on site depending on the westward adjustment outcome.

**Next steps:**

Submissions under Clause 52.20-4 consultation should focus on specific planning provisions: Planning Report errors (Clause 52.20-5 obligations), height precedent (largest GRZ override on register), incremental change classification (policy gap for apartments), mid-rise location direction (Clause 02.03-4), crossover safety (Clause 52.20-7.18 not assessed against underpass), Rutland Street interface, and missing wind assessment (Clause 52.20-7.21 unverifiable).

The Minister can require AS5100 assessment before deciding between two approaches: Tier 1 (6-7 storeys, Noone Street access, 4.0m Rutland setback) roughly triples existing housing without requiring structural assessment. Tier 2 (with westward adjustment if AS5100 assessment confirms feasibility) enables generous setbacks and more than triples existing housing. Both approaches improve planning outcomes and pedestrian safety.

**Key finding: every project of 9 storeys or taller is in a non-residential zone** (MUZ3, ACZ1, or C1Z). Tallest project in any residential zone is Wellington Street (Collingwood, 8 storeys, 26.4m, GRZ3).

#### The GRZ precedent question

**Methodological note:** Override multipliers calculated using current zoning and current height limits (as at February 2026). Zones and height limits may have changed since project approvals.

Four confirmed GRZ projects on register exceed zone height limit (using current zones and limits):

1. **1-12 Bills Street, Hawthorn** - Building G (tallest): 7 storeys, 22.5m (measured from highest natural ground level to roof level per north elevation), part of 6-building development, **GRZ4**. Boroondara GRZ4 schedule specifies no height, so 11m default applies. **2.05x the default height limit**.

2. **554-558 High Street Road, Mount Waverley** - 6 storeys, 18.6m (measured from highest natural ground level to building high point per [east elevation](https://spaces.hightail.com/space/vUdnDA0xzZ/files/fi-ae07ccbb-850e-42bd-9325-d64fc9f25286/fv-a43d59a5-089a-4363-a142-02362ada306c/01.%20Architectural%20Plans.pdf#pageThumbnail-10)), **GRZ2**. Monash GRZ2 has a 9m mandatory schedule limit (same as Yarra GRZ2). **2.07x the schedule height limit**.

3. **240 Wellington Street, Collingwood** - 8 storeys, 26.4m, **GRZ3**. GRZ3 in Yarra scheme is titled "Office of Housing Sites over 2500sqm & Selected Main Road Sites" - a schedule specifically for public housing land. No height specified in schedule; Clause 32.08 default of **11m mandatory** applies. **2.4x the default height limit**. Same municipality (Yarra) as Clifton Hill proposal.

4. **147-161 Elizabeth Street, North Richmond** - 27.06m (measured from level ground to Building 2 roof per plans), **GRZ3**. Same GRZ3 schedule as Wellington Street. No height specified in schedule; Clause 32.08 default of **11m mandatory** applies. **2.46x the default height limit**. Same municipality (Yarra) as Clifton Hill proposal.

**The Clifton Hill proposal would be the largest GRZ height override on the register.** Proposed 8 storey / 26.5m building is **2.94x the GRZ2 schedule limit of 9 metres**, 42% higher than the next closest override (Elizabeth Street, North Richmond at 2.46x). The two closest precedents, Elizabeth Street (2.46x) and Wellington Street (Collingwood, 2.4x), are both in GRZ3, a schedule specifically titled "Office of Housing Sites over 2500sqm & Selected Main Road Sites." High Street (Mount Waverley, 2.07x) is the only other GRZ2 project on the register. Bills Street (Hawthorn, 2.05x) is in GRZ4.

**Critical distinction:** Clifton Hill and High Street (Mount Waverley) exceed schedule-specified mandatory limits. Bills Street (Hawthorn), Elizabeth Street (North Richmond), and Wellington Street (Collingwood) exceed default limits that apply where schedules don't specify height. Clifton Hill's 2.94x override of a mandatory schedule limit is the largest on the register.

**A 6-7 storey alternative would fall within the precedent range:**
- 6 storeys: 2.07x (exactly matches High Street, Mount Waverley - the only other GRZ2 project on the register)
- 7 storeys: 2.50x (above Elizabeth Street, North Richmond, 2.46x, and Wellington Street, Collingwood, 2.4x)
